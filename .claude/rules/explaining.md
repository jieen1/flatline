# Explaining

Applies whenever you explain a rule, a gate, a process change, or a mechanism —
in chat, in a report, in a doc — to me or to anyone I hand it to. I am reading as
a manager, not at code level, unless I ask for more.

- **Explain the mechanism, not the artifact.** Never "the page contains X" /
  "the design lists Y". Say who does what, then what happens.
- **Every rule or gate gets four parts, every time:**
  1. the question the actor answers, or the action they take;
  2. each outcome and who acts on it — a small table when there are two to
     four discrete outcomes;
  3. what enforces it;
  4. the likely misreading, named and corrected ("PASS at design time does not
     mean delivered — the audit still checks the built system").
- **Define a term before you use it**, in one sentence, the first time it appears.
- **"Plain words" means the right altitude and the causal chain — not shorter
  sentences.** Compressing to a description is not simplifying.
- **Do this in reports and first answers**, not only when I ask a follow-up.
  Do not make me ask "is this a rule or a prerequisite?" to get the mechanism.
